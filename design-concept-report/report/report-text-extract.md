Notion AR: Turning 2D Notes into 3D Spatial Ideas

DECO2300 Design Concept Report | Meta Quest 3 AR Application Redesign

Application Redesign and Concept

This concept redesigns Notion as an augmented reality workspace for existing Notion users, including students, adult productivity users, designers, artists and architecture students. This redesign focuses on visual note taking transforming designs from 2D to 3D, specialising in early design exploration rather than trying to replace every Notion feature. The target place for running this will be the Meta Quest headset, using mixed reality passthrough, surface detection and hand tracking.

A user places a Notion note onto a real flat surface such as a table or a wall, sketches simple 2D shapes or design outlines they want to transform, then places both hands flat and lifts upward to “raise” the drawing into a 3D recreation. The object can then be pinched and repositioned within the AR workspace. The current concept will start small with things like shapes, easy drawings and adding the special movement, with more complex editing left for future development.

User Tasks and Goals

XR Interactions and Affordances

Surface detection for identifying usable surfaces for the note, making the digital workspace feel physical and understandable for the user.

The L-shaped hand gesture creates a quick document selection interaction that is specific to hand tracked AR.

The two hand lifting gesture makes the 2D to 3D transformation feel like the user is physically pulling the sketch out of the surface. Gesture tracking will be used to identify movements and gestures.

Pinch movement lets the user reposition and inspect the object in space.

Ideation and Prototype Refinement

The storyboard shows how the concept evolved. The first idea was a large wrap-around whiteboard that surrounded the user and could convert speech into writing. This was refined because it could look socially awkward, feel enclosed and make editing difficult especially in the air. The concept then shifted to a surface based workspace where the document is placed onto a surface and edited with a stylus along with hand interaction. This refinement made the idea more practical, more grounded in the user’s real environment and easier to connect to Notion documents. The final concept keeps the table workspace and adds the stronger XR interaction of raising a 2D drawing into a 3D object.

Initial Testing Plan

Participants would complete one scenario: make the L gesture, place a notion page, draw a simple shape, raise it into 3D and move it by pinching the object. Testing would focus on whether the 2D to 3D feature feels useful and whether the table makes the workspace easier to understand.
